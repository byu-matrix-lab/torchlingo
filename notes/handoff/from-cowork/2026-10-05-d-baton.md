## Two things not in the three files

**1. Encoder self-attention from the pretrained model, for tutorial 8 if you want it.** For
today's 8b deck I captured the pretrained English-to-Spanish model's *encoder self-attention*
(per layer, per head, and head-averaged) on "And that is very useful information." PyTorch's
encoder layers never return weights, so it is a forward pre-hook on each `TransformerEncoderLayer`
that re-runs `layer.self_attn(x, x, x, need_weights=True, average_attn_weights=...)` on the
layer's input, after `model.pos_encoding(model.src_tok_emb(src))`. Twelve lines; the weights are in
the course folder as `Figures/selfattn.json` (averaged, three layers) and
`Figures/selfattn_heads.json` (layer 0, four heads). Tutorial 8 shows only cross-attention today;
a "the same sentence, attending to itself" cell with the four heads of layer 0 side by side would
make the self-attention point the deck now makes with those pictures. Your call whether it earns
a place; if it does, `capture_self_attention` beside `capture_cross_attention` is the natural
home. Head-averaged maps on this model are diffuse; individual heads are sharp and not
interpretable, which is itself the lesson the deck draws.

**2. The pretrained model's facts, as the decks now state them.** From the checkpoint:
`d_model` 128, 4 heads, 3 + 3 layers, `d_ff` 512, 2,544,056 parameters, vocabulary 3,000 shared
pieces, 64,311 training pairs, 36 epochs, 24.3 minutes, final train loss 3.73, val 3.70, test set
1,889 pairs. The 8b deck has a slide on it; the 8a alignment slide and the Lecture 5 TED-corpus
slide now cite it too. If any of those numbers move (a retrain, a new corpus), say so in a hand-off
and the slides follow; `trained_minutes` and `train_pairs` in the checkpoint are what made this
easy, so please keep writing them.
